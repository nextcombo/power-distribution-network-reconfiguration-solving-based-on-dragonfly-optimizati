1. Overview of the expanding power system (SEP) to meet consumer demand for electrical energy. In this context, the role of the distribution system expansion planning (PESD) is to determine guidelines for the expansion of the distribution network. To this end, it is necessary to make changes to feeder components, such as replacing conductors exceeding load limits within the established planning scope, altering feeder configurations by changing switch states, and delivering essential elements of energy to customers that ensure continuity and quality. Given these circumstances, this work proposes a method for planning the distribution system as a nonlinear optimization problem that is solved using an heuristic dragonfly optimization algorithm, which will be developed in conjunction with OpenDSS for calculating power flows and Python for collecting, modifying feeders and displaying results. The dragonfly algorithm is responsible for reconfiguring the feeders, aiming to minimize both expansion costs and technical losses. The proposed method was tested on an adjusted IEEE 123-bus feeder, which is a test network containing over 123 nodes, multiple switches, regulators, transformers, etc. Finally, the reconfiguration scheme saved 22% compared to the original expansion plan, demonstrating the effectiveness of the algorithm when applied to distribution systems.

This paper proposes a solution to the problem of extending the distribution system considering minimization of technical losses and expansion costs. The approach involves reconfiguring feeders through the Dragonfly Algorithm (DA) algorithm, with power flow calculations performed by OpenDSS integrated with Python. The reconfiguration of feeders is determined heuristically inspired by the Dragonfly Optimization Algorithm. Testing networks for the proposed method were conducted on the adapted IEEE 123 bus feeder. Network extensions without reconfiguration are referred to as STO, while those with reconfiguration are termed STR. In the simulated executions, the heuristic was able to identify new configurations for switches within the feeders, thus achieving a good solution that minimized expansion costs by 22% compared to STO, with an average increase in technical losses of 5% compared to STO systems. The results obtained are significant for the extension planning method, allowing for the reconfiguration of branch lines to avoid reinforcement of the main branches according to the load standards set by the franchise company's work plan. Additionally, the planning method includes cost per kilometer, making the proposed model closer to reality. For the IEEE 123 node case, annual reconfiguration of feeders could avoid modifying three lines of the main branch network. This work also involved reusing removed conductors to reinforce other loop current-lower lines, thereby saving costs and only generating operational costs. Finally, the Dragonfly reconfiguration method observed a reduction in the active power of the feeders by 1%, and an increase in reactive power by 5%. Regarding the issue of distribution line load problems, this work applied reconductoring and reconfiguration techniques to maximize the cost savings from network reinforcement. However, other technologies can be applied to address load issues, such as increasing the voltage level of sections or constructing new substations. Therefore, the heuristic method proposed in this study can be applied to these issues, proven effective in finding global minimum points. Moreover, to make feeders closer to reality, the DA can be used to assign capacitor groups, converting technical losses into monetary values for better analysis of their representation.
